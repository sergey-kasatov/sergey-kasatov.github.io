How the tests work

Every model is tested alone on the box, over the network, on the 100 W charger, with nothing downloading. Speed comes from TiinyBench: tokens per second on one 1,500-token answer, how fast a 6,000-token prompt is read, and the load time. Quality is seven fixed prompts. Five are graded by a script, not by opinion: a pandas function and a SQL query are run on small test tables whose right answer is known (the SQL table has two equal amounts, so a rank that skips a number fails), a function with two bugs must pass four tests, a scrap-rate sum must give exactly 2,073 parts and EUR 25,705.20, and a job search must be sent as a correct tool call. That is the "x/5". Two answers are read and graded A to F: a German follow-up mail and a three-point Russian summary of a German meeting protocol with ten facts. Because one run can be luck, the three code tasks are asked twice more with different random seeds: three tasks times three runs is the "x/9". The tasks and test data are our own and new, so no model has seen them, and they are small, so they show what a model gets right, not a public leaderboard rank.

What it means

  • On the 100 W charger the base model runs 6 % above the reference (26.4 tok/s) and gemma sits inside the normal spread (−7 %).
  • Power was the main limit: on the 65 W adapter with the network dock attached, Turbo ran 18 % slow; on the 100 W charger it matches the reference exactly (32.5 tok/s). Run the box on the 100 W charger.
  • gpt-oss-20b gained 12 % on 100 W but stays 18 % below the reference while reading long prompts faster than it, which points at a changed model build (27 instead of 32 NPU units).
  • gemma reads long prompts a third slower than the reference on either charger, which again points at a changed model build.
  • Ornith-1.0-35B matches the reference (−3 %), and with thinking on it wrote the same 598-token answer as in the reference run.
  • Qwen3.5-9B reads prompts exactly as fast as the reference but generates 15 % slower, and it now takes 29 NPU units instead of 16, another sign of a changed model build.
  • Qwen3-Coder-30B-A3B-Instruct-Turbo generates 17 % slower at an unchanged NPU cost, and the cause is open. Its short answers still come out at 44-47 tok/s.
  • A re-run repeated both slow figures within 1 % (Qwen3.5-9B 17.8 tok/s, Coder 24.3 tok/s), so the 9B and Coder gaps are real on Unit 1 and this firmware, not run-to-run noise.
  • Qwen3-30B-A3B-Instruct generates 16 % slower than the reference, the same gap as the Coder Turbo and gpt-oss-20b, while the Qwen3.6 models and Ornith match it.
  • Embedding-4b and the reranker match the reference once they have had about 15 seconds after loading. Right after loading they answer with an empty reply, which TiinyBench counts as a failure. On the builds of 22 Sep (14 October) Embedding-4b and Nemotron-3-Embed-1B still did this, while the reranker answered at once.
  • A cancelled request keeps the box busy. On all seven embedding and reranker models, a request that the client cuts off after 5 seconds is still computed to the end: the next small request waits 25 to 39 seconds instead of 0.1 to 0.6. The builds of 13 Aug and 3 Sep (13 October) and the builds of 22 Sep (14 October) behave the same, within 2 seconds per model, although the update of 13 October lists a fix for background inference after a cancelled request. Tested by closing the connection from the client; no other way of cancelling was tried.
  • The newer models were measured after every download had finished. A download running on the box slows a benchmark, so no run starts while one is in progress.
  • The three utility models run 6 to 13 % behind the reference, the same spread as the chat models.
  • Z-Image-Turbo needs 8.4 seconds per 512 x 512 image. With the same seeds it wrote files of exactly the same size as the reference run, so the image model has most likely not changed since September. The 30 seconds seen in the app on 4 October was the time between saves, including typing the prompt.
  • The speech gap is 0.05 to 0.11 seconds per clip at every clip length, which points at a fixed delay per request rather than slower recognition.
  • Speech recognition gives a different transcript on every run, so a word-error figure is one sample. Setting the temperature to 0 does not change that: the endpoint ignores the setting. In one Russian pass it added a phrase that was never spoken ("computer vision systems and statistical analysis") and turned "SQL" into "SQLAlchemy". Read machine-made minutes before relying on them.
  • For speech on this box, Parakeet is the pick: about five times faster than Qwen3-ASR on its build of 28 Sep (six times on the build of 2 Aug) at similar German and Russian accuracy. Qwen3-ASR on the build of 28 Sep makes the fewest English errors of all (0.7-1.3 %). Whisper as served is the slowest and adds phrases that were not spoken.
  • For documents, the vision model Qwen3.6-35B-A3B is the best reader on the box: no errors on either page, Russian included, in 11-13 seconds a page. PP-OCRv6-Medium is three times faster for German and English but cannot read Cyrillic. GLM-OCR does not work through the API on this firmware.
  • gpt-oss-120b, the largest model in the store, takes the whole box and generates 16.8 tok/s, 11 % below the September figure, which was measured on an older, pruned build. Eight parallel calls share a flat 17 tok/s without errors. It suits slow background batches; for chat, Qwen3.6-35B-A3B is faster.
  • Qwen3.8-27B, the model owners rate highest for agents and careful coding, runs at 15.1 tok/s, a third faster than the September figure (11.4) and in line with the faster build the store shipped on 28 September. It is still half the speed of Qwen3.6-35B-A3B-Turbo, and parallel calls do not add throughput.
  • Qwen3-Coder-Next, Qwen3.5-35B-A3B and GLM-4.7-Flash match the reference. Coder-Next takes 94 of the 100 NPU units and two minutes to load, so it fills the box on its own.
  • Qwen3.6-27B-Turbo is slower than Qwen3.8-27B (11.1 against 15.1 tok/s), but the quality test below makes it worth keeping: it got every code task right in three runs and wrote the best German and Russian.
  • For speech output, Qwen3-CustomVoice reads German and English cleanly at 1.5x real time; its Russian has a strong accent. The voice clone model works, but only through request fields that are not in the vendor's documentation yet. supertonic-3 lost most of the text.
  • All embedding models found the right passage 8 to 10 times out of 10, across languages. The small 0.6B model is three to ten times faster than the larger ones, which matters when a whole vault has to be indexed.
  • Two small models loaded side by side (speech output plus speech recognition) failed once with a memory-style error, while each one alone worked.
  • For pictures with words in them, FLUX.2-klein-4B is the pick: it spelled the German test word correctly, umlauts included, where Z-Image broke it, and on its build of 18 Sep it is the fastest image model on the box (5.3 s per 512 x 512 image, 35 s at 1024 x 1024). ERNIE-Image-Turbo also spells correctly but takes twice as long at 1024 x 1024.
  • PP-OCRv6-Small reads real documents almost as well as Medium in half the time; both answer under the same internal name, so load only one of them. The layout model PP-DocLayoutV3 returns empty replies through the API, like GLM-OCR.
  • Images are not limited to 512 x 512: 1024 x 1024 and 1024 x 576 render natively in about 38 seconds, four times the 512 time.
  • Speed and quality are different questions. In the quality test only Qwen3-Coder-Next and Qwen3.6-27B-Turbo got the pandas, SQL and bug-fix tasks right in all three runs (9 of 9); gpt-oss-20b and Qwen3-30B-A3B-Thinking followed with 8, gemma with 7; Qwen3.6-35B-A3B and Qwen3.5-35B-A3B got 6, and the fast Qwen3.6-35B-A3B-Turbo (old build), Qwen3-Coder-30B, Qwen3.8-27B, gpt-oss-120b and the two small Qwen3 models 5 each. Qwen3-30B-A3B-Instruct got 3: the same pandas error and the same wrong rank in every run.
  • The most common miss is not reading the task closely: asked for ranks without gaps, ten of nineteen models used RANK instead of DENSE_RANK, and Turbo and Coder-30B did it in every run. Check generated SQL against the requirement, not only for errors.
  • For coding, Qwen3-Coder-Next is the pick: right every time and the fastest of the error-free models (66 s for all seven tasks), but it takes 94 of the 100 NPU units. For work that mixes code with German and Russian text, Qwen3.6-27B-Turbo is the careful choice at 11 tok/s; Qwen3.6-35B-A3B-Turbo is three times faster with the same text quality but slips on details.
  • Two models are unreliable for text: Qwen3-Coder-30B-Turbo invented facts in the Russian summary (cracks, a person who is not in the protocol, the wrong month), and Qwen3-4B answered the Russian task in German.
  • Without a seed the box can return the identical answer to the same request again (Qwen3-Coder-Next gave byte-identical code three times over three and a half hours), so a repeat test needs a different seed per run. With one, the same model wrote three different pandas solutions.
  • The dense Qwen3.6-27B matches the reference at 6.2 tok/s, which is too slow for chat. The non-Turbo Qwen3-Coder-30B runs 27.3 tok/s, faster than its Turbo on Unit 1 (24.4). The small Qwen3-8B and Qwen3-4B (18.9 and 17.7) are no faster than the 30B models, which only use about 3B parameters per word.
  • Fun-ASR-Nano transcribes English well (4.6 % word errors) and gives unusable mixed-language output for German and Russian. Its build of 28 Sep is faster (4.2-6.5x against 2.9-4.0x) and the same otherwise: English 5.9 %, German and Russian unusable. Fun-ASR-MLT-Nano on the build of 28 Sep reads German (5.9-10.5 % word errors, 16 % before) and returns Russian for the first time, with 31-44 % errors. The VoiceDesign speech model works once the voice is described in the not yet documented field instruct; its Russian is the least clear of all speech models.
  • The reranker put the right passage first for all ten questions, as well as the two best embedding models and better than the small fast ones (6 to 8). A small embedding model to find candidates, then the reranker to order them, is the setup to try for vault search.
  • Updating the three outdated factory models changed little: the new builds are as fast as the old ones once the slower afternoon is taken into account, and as accurate. The store does not show yet that a build is outdated; the box's own data does.
  • The three Z-Image style models do what their names say, about 11 % slower than plain Z-Image on their builds of 13 Aug and slightly faster than it on their builds of 22 Sep.
